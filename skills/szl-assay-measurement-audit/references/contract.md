# Assay measurement contract v1

This skill accepts one JSON object, at most 1 MiB. Duplicate keys, nonfinite numbers, unknown fields, symlinked inputs, and unsupported curves are errors (CLI exit 2). No network or credentials are used. The source JSON is read only; `--output` creates a new file exclusively.

Required top-level keys: `schema` (`szl.assay-measurement.v1`), `assay_id`, `run_id`, `concentration_unit`, `method_ref`, `standards_ref`, `limits`, `calibrators`, `blank`, `quality_controls`, `samples`. IDs are nonidentifying 1–64 character tokens. References are supplied labels or paths, not authenticated readbacks; empty references become `HOLD`. The same concentration unit is required on each calibrator and QC; the tool never converts units.

`limits` declares four numbers before looking at results: `max_abs_standard_residual` and `max_abs_blank_signal` in instrument-signal units, `max_qc_relative_error` as a fraction from 0 to 1, and `quantification_min` in the declared concentration unit. These are user-supplied method limits, not defaults or standards endorsed by SZL.

`calibrators` has 3–16 separately identified wells with distinct nonnegative concentrations and `signal`, with fields `well_id`, `concentration`, `unit`, `signal`. The helper fits the specified unweighted model `signal = intercept + slope × concentration` using Decimal arithmetic; slope must be positive. Every absolute standard residual must be at most its limit. `quantification_min` must lie within the calibration concentration range.

`blank` is one separately identified `well_id` and `signal`, with absolute signal at most its declared limit. `quality_controls` has 2–16 separately identified wells, each with `well_id`, `target_concentration`, `unit`, `signal`. Targets must be positive and within the calibration range. Recovered concentration is `(signal - intercept) / slope`; absolute relative error from target must be at most the declared fraction, and recovery must remain in range. Unique IDs do not prove physical independence. These checks are per-run observations, not a method-validation study.

`samples` has 1–256 entries, each with `sample_id`, `well_id`, `signal`, `dilution_factor` in 1..1e6, and optionally the complete uncertainty declaration: `expanded_uncertainty`, `coverage_factor`, `uncertainty_unit`, `uncertainty_basis`, and `dilution_uncertainty_included`. All well IDs across the run and all sample IDs must be unique. Missing uncertainty fields put that sample on `HOLD`; when present, expanded uncertainty must be positive, coverage factor at least 1, and the uncertainty unit must match. `uncertainty_basis` must equal `FINAL_REPORTED_CONCENTRATION`; when dilution factor exceeds one, `dilution_uncertainty_included` must be true. These are supplied declarations, not independently proven facts. The sample signal is inverted through the accepted calibration line, checked against its range and `quantification_min`, then multiplied by the declared dilution factor. The helper does not recalculate or verify the supplied expanded uncertainty, coverage factor, or dilution operation.

Numbers can be JSON numbers or quoted decimal strings; booleans, NaN/Infinity, magnitudes outside zero or 1e-30..1e30, and unsupported numeric representations are refused. Reports preserve Decimal strings at the calculation context's precision so closely spaced declared limits do not collapse in machine output; the digits do not establish analytical precision. The source declaration and user-selected limits must be reviewed outside this tool. A `PASS_DECLARED_CHECKS` result is not proof of sample identity, reference-material traceability, method fitness, uncertainty adequacy, scientific validity or clinical suitability. A requested output path must be on a filesystem that supports same-directory hard links; otherwise the CLI fails without publishing a partial report.
